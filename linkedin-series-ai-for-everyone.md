## Week 1 — Four Skills to Work With AI Without Losing Your Mind
*Article: https://edsonfjr.com/notes/four-skills-ai-adoption.html*

---

AI will make you sharper or more dependent. The tool you pick doesn't decide which. Your habits do.

Most people think AI adoption is a software problem.

Pick the right tool. Learn the right prompts. Get faster.

It's not. It's a thinking problem.

A study from MIT found that people who used AI for writing had lower brain connectivity, weaker memory recall, and less sense of ownership of their own work. The tool did the task. The person got worse at it.

I've thought about this a lot — because I use AI every day, and I want to use it in a way that makes me sharper, not less capable.

Here's what I've landed on: there are four skills that determine whether AI amplifies your thinking or slowly replaces it.

They're not AI skills. They're thinking skills that AI makes non-negotiable.

I wrote them down so they'd be useful beyond me.

## Week 2 — The Cockpit Rule: An AI Decision Framework
*Article: https://edsonfjr.com/notes/ai-cockpit-framework.html*

---

Most people use AI the same way for every task. That's why it feels underwhelming half the time.

A pilot doesn't let autopilot run during takeoff and landing.
During cruise, they hand it over.
In an emergency, they take full manual control.

Same plane. Three different modes. The pilot knows which one fits the moment.

That's exactly how I think about AI now.

Not every task needs the same level of involvement from you. Some things AI can handle end to end. Some require real collaboration. Some need you to put the AI aside entirely.

The mistake most people make: they stay in "collaboration mode" for everything — working with AI, reviewing every output, second-guessing every suggestion. That's the wrong gear for tasks AI can handle fully. And not enough gear for decisions that need your judgment.

I built a simple 3-question framework to pick the right mode before starting a task. It takes 30 seconds and saves a lot of wasted effort.

I called it the Cockpit Rule.

## Week 3 — 5 Layers of AI Tooling: From Vibe Coding to Autonomous Agents
*Article: https://edsonfjr.com/notes/ai-tooling-layers.html*

---

Stop asking which AI tool is the best one. It's the wrong question.

The right question is: how much of the loop do you want to keep?

There are five layers of AI tooling — from tools that need you to drive every step, to agents that run entire workflows on their own. Each layer has a different risk profile, a different skill requirement, and a different kind of output.

Most people compare tools without realizing they're comparing tools from different layers.

A tool that helps you build by describing what you want and an autonomous agent that runs a whole goal end to end are not competing. They're solving different problems at different levels. Comparing them is like comparing a power drill to a construction crew.

Once you see the five layers, you stop asking "which is better" and start asking "which layer do I need for this job."

That shift alone changes how you evaluate every AI tool you'll ever come across.

## Week 5 — How I Built an AI Agent Workflow and Stayed in Control
*Article: https://edsonfjr.com/notes/ai-agents-human-in-control.html*

---

I built an AI agency for myself. Four agents. I make every final call.

Most people talk about AI agents like they're science fiction. I've been running one for months.

Here's what it actually looks like:

One agent captures ideas and organizes them. One writes the first draft of every note I publish. One updates the site structure when something goes live. I review everything before it ships. I press the commit button manually. Every time.

The agents accelerate the mechanics. I make the decisions.

That's what human oversight looks like in practice — not a safety slogan, a real workflow where you stay in the loop at the moments that matter.

This last post in the series is also the most personal. It's what happened when I stopped treating AI as a helper and started treating it as a system I designed and actually control.

The tools cost nothing. The design cost time. It was worth it.
